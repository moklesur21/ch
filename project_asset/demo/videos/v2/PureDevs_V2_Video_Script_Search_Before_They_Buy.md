## HOOK + PROBLEM — 0:00–0:30

**[ON CAMERA, founder]**

When someone searches your store and finds what they were looking for, ninety-two percent of them buy it. Seventy-eight percent buy something else on the way out.

**[GRAPHIC: stat card — Google Cloud / The Harris Poll, 2023]**
> *On screen (9 words):* **Successful search: 92% buy it. 78% buy more.**

That's Google Cloud and the Harris Poll. Which makes the search box the highest-intent surface in your entire store. **[PAUSE]**

Here's the part nobody talks about. In a standard WooCommerce install, it's also the only surface that keeps no record of what happened on it.

Your order report is a complete record of what sold. It's a complete record of nothing else. The demand that typed itself into your search box, opened three product pages and left without buying — that isn't *missing* from the report. It was never in scope.

## DAMAGE / CONTEXT — 0:30–2:00

**[VOICEOVER + B-ROLL]**

*Transition:* So how much sits outside that scope?

Search abandonment — someone searches, doesn't find it, leaves — costs retailers more than two trillion dollars a year globally. More than two hundred and thirty-four billion in the United States alone. Same research.

**[B-ROLL: a shopper types into a store search bar, pauses, closes the tab]**

I'm not going to pretend a neat slice of that is yours. The number matters for its shape, not its size. It's the cost of an event that by definition produced no order — so it produced no row in any report built on orders.

And you can't tell which side of it you're on.

**[GRAPHIC: stat card — Baymard Institute, 2026]**
> *On screen (8 words):* **56% of sites: "mediocre or worse" search UX**

Baymard's 2026 benchmark puts fifty-six percent of ecommerce sites at mediocre-or-worse for search experience. Half the room has a problem. Without the actual search terms, no individual owner can tell whether they're in that half. They can only tell that sales are what they are.

**[VOICEOVER + B-ROLL: product page with a title that doesn't match a handwritten note beside it]**

And a version of this keeps coming up in WooCommerce groups — an owner finds out by accident that customers search for their product using a word that appears nowhere on the site. Not in the title. Not in the category. Nowhere. **[PAUSE]**

That word was demand. It arrived. It got nothing back.

## PRODUCTION NOTES

### The rule that governs this demo

**No spoken figures anywhere in the demo.** The narration says *"read the proportion, not the number."* It does double duty: it works on any demo store regardless of what the data says on recording day, and it's rhetorically stronger than reciting totals.

Point at **shapes, ranks, gaps and trends.**

| ✅ Say | ❌ Never say |
|---|---|
| "Running at roughly twice the one below it" | "Forty-six searches for 'watch'" |
| "Near the top of one list, nowhere near the top of the other" | "A hundred and fifteen views on the Everyday Tee" |
| "Watch which words are climbing" | "Up from twelve last month" |

The two stat cards are unaffected — those are verified external figures, spoken with organisation and year. The rule governs **demo-store numbers in narration** only.

### Guardrail check — all clear

- **Observe / report / predict only.** Nothing in this script sends, recovers, wins back, discounts, automates or changes store data. The shape used is "the plugin surfaces it; you act on it."
- **Stage discipline.** Only 3.0.0 shipped capability appears. Zero-result and searched-never-purchased reporting are Release 1.5 / Release 2 and are **absent by design** — do not add them in the edit.
- **Competitive claims.** No "only," "first," or "nobody else." The FiboSearch beat is deliberate ALSO-HAVE framing: a verifying prospect will find those plugins within one search, and hearing it from you first is worth more than the sentence costs.
- **Positioning shape.** "WooCommerce shows you what sold. PureDevs adds ___, so you can decide ___." Never "WooCommerce has no analytics."
- **Accuracy boundary spoken on camera:** a search is a behavioural signal, not proven intent — and no non-purchase is labelled lost revenue.
- **No prices, no filter counts, no RFM segment count** — none are needed in this topic.
- **No "subscribe," no email-list ask.** Plugin link lives in the description.
- **Freshness (checked 25 July 2026):** WooCommerce core 10.9.4 stable; 11.0 GA 28 July. Neither ships on-site search-term reporting — the 11.0 reporting work is Analytics import retry, refund values in v3 sales-report buckets, and stable visitor IDs. The claim in this script is **"not in core,"** never "impossible in WooCommerce." Re-confirm on recording day if that slips past 28 July.

### Pre-record checklist

1. **Fill the menu path.** Confirm the exact wp-admin label for the search page and update every `[VERIFY LABEL]` marker. The script names paths out loud — this is the one thing a viewer can check against their own install.
2. **Confirm "Most Viewed Brands"** exists in 3.0.0, or leave it out of the flow entirely (it isn't in the shipped feature list).
3. **Seed the demo store** so the top search term is genuinely *not* a catalogue word. The whole middle of the demo depends on that one term existing.
4. **Pick the viewed-heavily / sold-lightly product in advance** and know which honest explanation you'd give for it.
