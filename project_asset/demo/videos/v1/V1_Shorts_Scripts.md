## SHORT 2 — "What a customer health score actually measures"

**Sanity checks:** 1 ✅ · 2 ✅ · 3 ✅ · 4 ✅ (see scale note) · 5 ✅ teaches the concept at base level; Short 4 walks one profile's components — different screens, different jobs · 6 ✅

### HOOK — 0:00–0:03

**On-screen text:** `What's a health score?`

**Spoken:** "Customer health score. Sounds vague. It isn't — here's exactly what it's made of."

### PROOF — 0:03–0:42

**[SCREEN CLIP: `Customer History → Dashboard`, Health Distribution. Zoomed crop for vertical — the full dashboard will not read at 9:16. What the viewer should notice: the **shape** of the base, and which way the weight is leaning.]**

**Spoken:** "Every customer gets scored out of a hundred, from their own behaviour. Three things drive it.

Recency — how long since they last ordered, measured against their own rhythm, not a calendar rule.

Frequency — whether the gap between orders is widening.

And whether the pattern they'd established is still holding.

Group them and you get the shape of your customer base. Don't read the totals. Look at where the weight sits — and which way it's moving."

### LANDING — 0:42–0:50

**Spoken:** "It's not a mood ring. It's recency, frequency and pattern, in one number."

**On-screen end card:** `Recency · Frequency · Pattern`

### Caption text

`Customer health score.` · `Sounds vague. It isn't.` · `Scored out of a hundred.` · `From their own behaviour.` · `Recency — against their own rhythm.` · `Not a calendar rule.` · `Frequency — is the gap widening?` · `Is their pattern still holding?` · `Don't read the totals.` · `Look where the weight sits.` · `Recency, frequency and pattern.` · `In one number.`

> **Scale-vs-figure note for the editor:** "scored out of a hundred" is the **scale definition**, not a demo-store value. It is permitted. Reading any actual score off the screen is not. If the edit needs a number on screen, use the words `0–100` as a graphic — never a customer's score.

## SHORT 4 — "Churn prediction: signal, not certainty"

**Sanity checks:** 1 ✅ · 2 ✅ · 3 ✅ · 4 ✅ · 5 ✅ the only short about the limits of the feature · 6 ✅ — and this short *is* the accuracy boundary, stated out loud

### HOOK — 0:00–0:03

**On-screen text:** `It can't tell the future.`

**Spoken:** "Churn prediction doesn't tell you who's leaving. Anyone promising that is selling you something."

### PROOF — 0:03–0:42

**[SCREEN CLIP: `Customer History → Churn Prediction` → open one at-risk profile → annotate the risk components. What the viewer should notice: these are **behaviours that already happened**, not a forecast.]**

**Spoken:** "Here's what it actually does. It reads behaviour that already happened.

How far past their own rhythm someone has drifted. Whether the gap between orders is widening. Whether a reorder that should have landed, didn't.

That's a signal, not a verdict. No date. No guarantee. It flags risk earlier than a revenue chart would — and that's the whole claim."

**Spoken:** "PureDevs Customer History flags it. You decide what to do about it. It doesn't email anyone on your behalf."

**On-screen end card:** `It flags. You act.`

### Caption text

`It can't tell the future.` · `Anyone promising that is selling something.` · `It reads behaviour that already happened.` · `How far past their own rhythm.` · `Is the gap between orders widening?` · `Did an expected reorder not land?` · `Then it ranks who's most at risk.` · `A signal, not a verdict.` · `No date. No guarantee.` · `It flags. You act.`
