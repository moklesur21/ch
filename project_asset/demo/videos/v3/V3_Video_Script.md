## HOOK + PROBLEM — 0:00–0:30 · [ON CAMERA, founder]

Two customers placed the same sixty-dollar order on your store this morning. [PAUSE]

In your order list, they are identical. Same total. Same status. Same kind of row. [PAUSE]

One is a first-time buyer you may never see again. The other has twenty-one orders behind that one — and what they do next matters more to your quarter than most of your ad spend. [PAUSE]

Your order list cannot tell you which is which. [PAUSE] Every order looks equal. Your customers are not.

## DAMAGE / CONTEXT — 0:30–2:00 · [VOICEOVER + B-ROLL]

*[B-ROLL: wp-admin tab-hopping — orders screen, a second analytics plugin, a spreadsheet scroll. Generic footage; never a staged "real post."]*

So what does that blind spot actually cost?

A version of this keeps showing up in WooCommerce groups. The morning check: the revenue widget, then a second plugin for another number, then a spreadsheet export to answer anything about an actual person. Ten minutes later there's sort of an answer — and still no idea who the store's most valuable customers are.

**[GRAPHIC: stat card — Harvard Business Review / Bain, 2014 — "Retention +5% → profits +25–95%"]**

Harvard Business Review, citing Bain: increase customer retention by five percent, and profits rise between twenty-five and ninety-five percent. It's the most quoted line in retention marketing. Here's the part nobody quotes: the research is about keeping the *right* customers. And you can't keep the right customers if you can't see who they are.

They're also a smaller group than most owners think.

**[GRAPHIC: stat card — Gorgias, 2023 — "21% of customers → 44% of revenue"]**

Across more than ten thousand merchants, repeat buyers were about a fifth of the customer base — and close to half the revenue. [PAUSE]

Owners keep describing the same moment of relief when a lifetime-value column finally appears next to each name — the first time the list separates the VIPs from the one-off bargain hunters. Because until that moment, a Champion and a coupon-chaser are the same row.

That's value blindness. [PAUSE] Not a missing report — a missing distinction.

## REMEDY / DEMO — 2:00–5:00 · [SCREEN RECORDING — Northline Supply Co. demo store]

> **EDITOR + FOUNDER NOTE (U7, binding): from here to the end of the recording, zero figures are spoken. Point at shapes, ranks, gaps, and bar lengths. Demo totals appear on screen only. Confirm exact wp-admin menu labels on the demo store before recording (Feature Reference Card).**

So what does it look like when your customer list finally makes that distinction?

This is a live WooCommerce demo store — Northline Supply Co. — with PureDevs Customer History installed. Everything on this screen is read from data the store already collects.

**[SCREEN RECORDING: Customer History → Customers & Users]**
Open Customer History, then Customers and Users. Here's the customer list — every buyer, one row each. And right now it's exactly as blind as your order list.

**[SCREEN RECORDING: apply the RFM filter → Champions]**
Now the filter. PureDevs scores every customer on recency, frequency, and monetary value, and sorts them into ten named segments — Champions, Loyal, Potential Loyalist, all the way down to Lost. Filter to Champions. [PAUSE]

**[GRAPHIC: soft highlight sweeping the filtered list and its spend column]**
The whole list collapses to a short column of names. And don't read the totals — read the proportion. Look at how much of the spend column sits inside this small group. That's this store's own concentration, measured right here — not a law borrowed from someone's slide deck. Yours will look different. But you have one.

**[SCREEN RECORDING: hold on the list columns]**
And look what now sits on every row: spend, health score, lifecycle stage — side by side. One glance tells you who's valuable, whether that value is rising or fading, and where the relationship stands.

**[SCREEN RECORDING: scroll to Marcus Hale next to a New Customer with a matching last-order value]**
Remember the two identical orders from the start? [PAUSE] Here they are. Two customers, last orders of near-identical value. In the order list — twins. In this list… **[GRAPHIC: circle the two rows]** look at the gap between their rows.

**[SCREEN RECORDING: open both profiles side by side — financial summaries + RFM bars]**
Open both profiles. Financial summary against financial summary, RFM bars against RFM bars. **[GRAPHIC: arrow on the distance between the two bar sets]** Read the shape, not the figures: one relationship is long, steady, and climbing. The other has barely begun. Same order — completely different customer. [PAUSE] And to be clear about what these badges are: they're read from what each customer has actually done. Signals, not certainties. But signals you've never had on one screen.

Here's the honest reason this plugin needs to exist. **WooCommerce shows you each customer's order count, total spend, and average order value — genuinely useful. PureDevs adds RFM segments, health, and lifecycle on the same record, so you can decide which relationships get your retention effort first.** Value stops being a spreadsheet project and becomes a screen.

One note on tiers, because honesty matters here: this customer list and these profiles are in the free plugin. The intelligence layer you're watching — RFM, health, lifecycle — is Pro.

**[SCREEN RECORDING: Save as Segment → "Champions" · then the Segments page showing the saved list beside the ready-made segments]**
Last click. Save this filtered view as a segment and name it Champions. The free plugin includes one saved segment; Pro removes the limit and ships ready-made segments — including High Value At Risk: the valuable customers who are already drifting. [PAUSE] And that's the result. Not a settings page — a named list of the relationships your store actually runs on.
